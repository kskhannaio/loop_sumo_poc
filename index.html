Madiwala loop: how the data flows

A tactical-urbanism study of the loop of 100 Feet Road, Hosur Road and Sarjapura Road in Bengaluru. Three repositories turn PDFs, maps and bus timetables into a study website, QGIS templates for planners, and simulation reports.

The chart could not load. In words: the project PDFs and OpenStreetMap go into the PDF pipeline, which exports a layer for the website, a GeoPackage for the QGIS templates, and the traffic tables. The simulation repo builds the QGIS templates and the SUMO model (with OpenStreetMap and the bus timetable). Proposals drawn on the website's Draw tab or in QGIS become simulation scenarios, and the results become reports; today's results also go back to the website.

The three repositories

cad_drawings_extraction

PDF pipeline. Given the PDFs, ends with finished files in exports/.

  • python main.py runs every step
  • exports/website/plans.geojson
  • exports/qgis/corridor_drawings.gpkg and sheets
  • exports/od/ traffic tables

corridor_design_challenge

The study website, a single map page with tabs.

  • Open the website
  • scripts/build_*.py make its data files
  • Draw tab saves a proposal file for the simulation

loop_sumo_poc

Traffic and pedestrian simulation, QGIS templates and reports.

  • python run.py builds and runs the model
  • scripts/make_proposal.py turns a drawing into a scenario
  • qgis/ builds the planner templates

Reports

  • Junction C proposal test: the planner's drawing for junction C against today's streets, with pedestrian results, videos and assumptions.
